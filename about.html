    • About the Publication

    Writing about space with intention, clarity, and patience.

    Dalmere Form Theory is an independent editorial project examining the principles that shape meaningful interiors and architecture.

    Dalmere Form Theory began in 2019 as a response to a gap in design publishing. Most outlets covering interior design and architecture operate on a cycle of novelty: new projects, new trends, new products, presented at speed and often reduced to a single striking photograph. We wanted to build something slower and more durable—a publication that treats design as a subject worthy of long-form consideration.

    Our name reflects the two halves of our interest. Form is what we can see and touch: the shape of a room, the placement of a window, the grain of a material, the way light falls across a wall at four in the afternoon. Theory is what lies beneath: the reasons those forms work, the principles that guide good decisions, the frameworks that help us evaluate quality rather than merely respond to style.

    What We Publish

    Every article on Dalmere Form Theory is developed over several weeks, with careful attention to accuracy, usefulness, and clarity. We publish three broad categories of writing:

    • Long-form essays — Considered arguments about the principles of design: restraint, proportion, light, materiality, and the psychology of space.
    • Practical guides — Actionable frameworks for homeowners and professionals: space planning standards, lighting layers, material selection, renovation strategy.
    • Case studies and field notes — Close readings of specific buildings, interiors, and details that reward sustained attention.

    We do not publish trend reports, product roundups, or listicles. Our writing is intended to remain useful for years, not weeks.

    Editorial Independence

    Dalmere Form Theory does not publish sponsored content, advertorials, or paid placements. We do not accept payment for coverage, and we do not allow commercial relationships to influence what we write or how we write it.

    This independence is central to what we do. It allows us to write critically when criticism is warranted, to praise work that deserves praise regardless of its marketing budget, and to maintain the distance that honest design writing requires.

    "The role of the critic is not to decorate the work, but to understand it—and to help others understand it too."

    Our Principles

    01

    Slowness over speed

    We publish two to three articles per month, not per day. Each piece is researched, written, and edited with care.

    02

    Principles over trends

    We write about the underlying ideas that make spaces work, not the surface styles that come and go.

    03

    Clarity over cleverness

    Design writing is often needlessly obscure. We aim for prose that is precise, readable, and free of jargon.

    04

    Independence over access

    We do not trade favorable coverage for access, invitations, or advertising. Our only obligation is to our readers.

    Who Writes for Us

    Dalmere Form Theory is led by founding editor Elias Dalmere, who spent fifteen years in practice as an architect and interior designer before turning to writing full-time. Elias writes the majority of our long-form essays and guides.

    We also work with a small group of contributing writers—architects, designers, material scientists, and historians—who bring specialist knowledge to specific subjects. Every piece, regardless of author, is edited to the same standard of accuracy and clarity.

    Who We Write For

    Our readers are homeowners planning renovations, design professionals seeking deeper frameworks, students of architecture and interiors, and general readers who are simply curious about why some spaces feel right and others don't.

    You do not need a design background to read Dalmere Form Theory. You need only an interest in the built environment and a willingness to think about it more slowly than most publications allow.
